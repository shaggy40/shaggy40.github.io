2026 · GRAYQUEST

An early warning system for a ₹80.76 Cr loan book

Collections was reactive — a parent missed an instalment, then someone called. I built a probability-of-default scorecard on 24,626 applications, staged the live book under Ind AS 109, and turned the scores into a ranked worklist sorted by expected loss. Logistic regression over a tree ensemble on purpose: a credit officer has to tell a parent why they were declined, and a coefficient does that where a black box can't.

The finding that mattered most wasn't statistical. 233 repeat defaulters were holding live loans and only 23% had been through a personal discussion before re-approval — a process gap fixable in a week, with no model required.

Why three gas distributors cost different money

I estimated the cost of capital for three listed City Gas Distribution firms expecting capital structure to explain the spread. It didn't — the difference traced almost entirely to equity beta, the market's read on each firm's regulatory and volume risk. At an 18% hurdle rate you reject projects a 14.4% peer would fund.

2026 · ACADEMIC

What Cipla is worth depends on how you end the forecast

A three-statement analysis of Cipla across FY2024–26, then a five-year FCFF model discounted at a CAPM-derived 9.72% WACC, triangulated against Sun Pharma and Dr. Reddy's on five multiples. FY2026 was a soft year — net profit fell 26.7% while total assets grew 13.7% — so most of the ratio story is an asset base compounding about six times faster than revenue, with goodwill and intangibles up ₹1,762 Cr on an acquisition.

The finding that mattered was a disagreement inside my own model. Gordon Growth put the share at ₹1,053 and an exit multiple at ₹1,570 — a 49% spread on identical cash flows, against a market price of ₹1,459. The gap is capex: year-five free cash flow is only 37% of EBITDA, so capitalising FCFF says overvalued and capitalising EBITDA says undervalued. Which number you believe is a view on whether the capex normalises, not a choice of technique.

How I work

Three things I believe about models

A number isn't an answer.

ROC-AUC of 0.97 changes nothing on its own. What changed at GrayQuest was a collections team getting a ranked list they could work through on a Monday morning.

Explainability is a requirement, not a preference.

A credit officer has to justify a rejection under RBI's Fair Practices Code. A model that can't be explained can't be deployed, whatever it scores.

Say what your model can't do.

Mine trains on a proxy target in a benign credit cycle. Against real defaults, expect closer to 0.80. Naming that is what makes the rest worth believing.

Background

Computer science first, finance second.

I did a B.Tech in computer science and then went looking for problems where the code was the easy part. Credit risk turned out to be exactly that: the modelling is well-trodden, and the hard question is always what a business should do with a number once it has one.

It's the fastest way I know to learn a product, and a business model.

I'm now in the second year of my PGDM at Great Lakes, Gurgaon, heading for analytics and risk roles in financial services. Outside coursework I compete in inter-collegiate and national case competitions, and I've taught programming to a class of thirty — still the fastest way to find out whether you actually understand something.
